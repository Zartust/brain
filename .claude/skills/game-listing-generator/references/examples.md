# Approved Meetup Description Examples

Real published descriptions from Steel City RPG events. Use to calibrate
tone, structure, length, and closer format. Do not copy — use to understand
what works and why.

Each entry includes: the published copy, the register/genre classification,
and annotated notes on what the listing does well or is missing.

## Dread
**Register:** Dread / Atmospheric + Social/Roleplay-Heavy
**Format:** GM-led

> Dread is a diceless, numberless horror game that emphasizes collaborative
> storytelling and immersive role-playing.
>
> Your character sheet is your answers to 13 (sometimes loaded) questions,
> and you and your party will be the protagonists of a one-shot horror story
> from which many of you may not make it out alive. Conflicts are resolved by
> pulling blocks from a Jenga tower — and if the tower falls, your character
> falls with it.
>
> Mechanics are simple and will be explained at the table; no previous
> experience is needed.
>
> The story you will play will be chosen by your party from a variety of
> options offered at the start of play.
>
> Survival horror? Gothic mystery? A slasher story? A dark faerie tale?
> So many ways to meet your fate.
>
> Check out other events happening for RPG Game Day.

**What works:**
- Jenga mechanic is a genuine hook — unique and immediately visual
- Genre-diversity tease at the close works well for a system that runs many story types
- Accessibility is woven into the body, not bolted on

**What's missing:**
- No atmospheric hook at the top — starts with an orientation sentence
- Closer has no tailored lead-in (plain fallback)
- No party frame or premise — works here because the story is chosen at table, but noting the absence

**Structural pattern:** Orientation → Mechanics → Accessibility → Genre tease → Closer

## Rise of the Redscales — D&D 5E
**Register:** Lighthearted / Comedy + Kinetic / Action
**Format:** GM-led, 4th-level characters

> What happens when the dragon queen doesn't come home — and the kobolds
> inherit the hoard?
>
> Welcome to Rise of the Redscales, a D&D 5E one-shot for an all-kobold
> party of 4th-level characters, set deep inside an enormous dragon's lair
> overflowing with treasure, traps, and very poor decisions.
>
> The Dragon Queen has been gone far too long. Her lair, once a place of
> order, fear, and strict hierarchy, has become a powder keg of greed,
> ambition, and unresolved grudges. Two kobold tribes — the Redscales and
> their long-hated rivals, the Bluescales — are on the brink of open war,
> and the entire lair is about to become their battlefield.
>
> You'll play the best and brightest of the Redscales: lifelong minions,
> trap-tenders, and hoard-polishers suddenly thrust into leadership,
> diplomacy, and all-out chaos.
>
> All hail the Redscales. Probably. Until something explodes.
>
> No experience needed. Pre-generated characters provided.

**What works:**
- Question hook is perfectly calibrated for comedy — immediate premise, immediate voice
- Character level in orientation is natural, not clinical
- Premise and party frame are the strongest section — named factions, specific world detail
- Comic closing line lands before accessibility and sets the tone for the whole table
- "Pre-generated characters provided" as a standalone sentence is clean and confident

**What's missing:**
- No tailored closer lead-in — should have one (e.g. "Not feeling the kobold life?")

**Structural pattern:** Question hook → Orientation with level → Premise → Party frame → Comic close → Accessibility → (closer missing)

## Ringmaster
**Register:** Intimate / Drama
**Format:** GM-less
**System engine:** Descended from the Queen

> The circus never forgets. It never releases its own.
>
> RINGMASTER is a GM-less, prompt-driven story game that plays with no prep
> or prior experience required.
>
> Through prompts, we'll discover how you joined this dark, supernatural
> circus, and what binds you to the troupe and its enigmatic Ringmaster.
> Is this your sanctuary — or your prison?
>
> When an angry mob gathers outside the Big Top with torches raised, the
> choice becomes clear: will you defend your home — or watch it burn?
>
> GM-less, collaborative storytelling, no prior experience needed.
>
> Want a different circus? Check out other events happening for RPG Game Day.

**What works:**
- Best overall structure in the set — every section earns its place
- Atmospheric hook doubles as the poster hook — unified voice across formats
- GM-less format is the first thing in the orientation — correct prioritization
- Premise and closing stakes are separated cleanly — setup then consequence
- Inline tag line instead of bullets — correct for GM-less/collaborative format
- Closer lead-in is game-specific and impossible to genericize

**What's missing:** Nothing significant. This is the reference standard.

**Structural pattern:** Atmospheric hook → GM-less orientation → Premise → Closing stakes → Inline tag → Closer

## The Expanse RPG
**Register:** Slow Burn / Political Intrigue + Drama
**Format:** GM-led

> Step into a near-future solar system where Earth, Mars, and the Belt are
> locked in a cold war — and one wrong move can shift the balance of power.
>
> In this one-shot set in the universe of The Expanse, you'll play a crew
> caught in the middle of something bigger than yourselves: political tension,
> corporate secrets, or a job that goes sideways fast. Hard science fiction,
> tough choices, and pressure from all sides.
>
> No experience with the system is required. Pre-generated characters will
> be provided — just show up, pick a role, and strap in.
>
> Want more fantasy? Check out other events happening for RPG Game Day.

**What works:**
- Most efficient listing in the set — delivers everything in under 100 words
- Party frame and orientation are merged into a single sentence without losing clarity
- "Just show up, pick a role, and strap in" — accessibility line that still has register
- Closer lead-in pivots on genre, which is smart for a sci-fi game at a fantasy-heavy event

**What's missing:**
- No closing lines before accessibility — could use one punchy imperative
- GM self-assessment language removed correctly (was in original submission)

**Structural pattern:** Scene-setting hook → Party frame/orientation merged → Accessibility → Closer

## Edirne — D&D 5E
**Register:** Kinetic / Action + Combat-Heavy
**Format:** GM-led

> The port city of Edirne is under siege.
>
> Corsair ships slam into the harbor, raiders flooding the streets as
> lightning tears across the sky. A terrible truth soon becomes clear —
> the corsairs are not acting alone.
>
> A blue dragon circles above the city, its thunderous roar shaking the
> docks as it prepares to strike.
>
> Can your party defend Edirne, repel the raiders, and survive the dragon's
> assault?
>
> A fast-paced D&D 5e one-shot with tactical combat. Miniatures provided
> for key encounters.
>
> No experience required. Pre-generated characters available.

**What works:**
- Scene-in-motion hook — drops directly into action
- Escalating revelation structure (corsairs → bigger threat) creates momentum
- Rhetorical question closing is effective for action register
- Minis reframed as a game feature, not a GM logistics note

**What's missing:**
- No tailored closer lead-in — needs one
- No link-back line at all — must always be present

**Structural pattern:** Scene hook → Escalating premise → Closing stakes (rhetorical) → System/feature note → Accessibility → (closer missing)

## Shadowdark — Bittermold Keep
**Register:** Classic Adventure / Dungeon Crawl + Dread
**Format:** GM-led, 1st-level characters

> Long ago, a fissure in the caverns below Bittermold Keep bubbled and grew,
> spewing forth a pool of muttering madness.
>
> This primordial ooze — Magdulbulb — drove the Bittermold family to
> insanity. Now, its petitioners zealously feed and protect it, warring with
> each other for their patron's favor.
>
> Shadowdark is a grimdark OSR one-shot for first-level characters. Expect
> danger from the first room.
>
> No experience needed.

**What works:**
- Lore-first opening creates genuine atmosphere
- Named antagonist (Magdulbulb) and named location give the scenario texture
- "Expect danger from the first room" is a strong closer-within-orientation

**What's missing:**
- No atmospheric hook above the lore — starts mid-story without a framing sentence
- Orientation is buried (third paragraph) — should come earlier
- No closing lines, no tailored closer, no link-back line
- Accessibility line is bare minimum — no pre-gen mention, no register texture
- This listing is underwritten and would benefit significantly from a full pass

**Structural pattern:** Lore premise → Lore continuation → Orientation/system → Accessibility → (missing: closing lines, closer)
